# What's OSINT?
OSINT stands for **O**pen **S**ource **Int**elligence.
It involves finding and analyzing information from publicly available sources like social media.

It's used by journalists, law enforcement and unfortunetaly bad actors such as stalkers to gather information about people.

This is **NOT** a data privacy guide about VPNs and fancy browser extensions, but a guide how to visibly protect yourself from **being doxxed**.
However please note that this guide is for an **average person**. If your life or safety is in danger, you will need way stronger measures.

I believe that every person should share as little information about themselves online as possible so you can't just tell where they live from a simple Google search.

# Why should I care?
Well... if you don't, go ahead and post your full name, phone number, home address and social security number (or whatever ID number your country uses, ex. PESEL). Also unlock your phone and give it to a random person on the street.

That's what you may be accidentally doing, except it takes a bit more effort to get this info.

Also if you don't care now, you may want to protect your identity in the future and then regret all the data you've shared that can't be now deleted.

# Common advice
This is generic starter advice that you'll likely see in other guides, but I want to bring it up before we get to the more specific advice.

Don't tell random people your address or anything you consider private.

Remember that once you post something online it may be too late to undo it, even if you delete it.
Someone could've already saved it, even automatically the second you posted it.

Keep your online and real life profiles separate.
Your real life friends can know that you are "Coolboy123", but your followers should not know who "Coolboy123" is. 

# Media
Photos and videos are more dangerous than you think.
By analyzing the environment, you can find the exact location where it was taken, even if it's censored or has seemingly irrelevant information.

This YouTube video: [how to find a street in 2 minutes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rl2Q9xH8e7M) by [RAINBOLT](https://www.youtube.com/@georainbolt) demonstrates it.

Sharing your face is dangerous as it uniquely identifies you and cannot be changed (most of the time).
If you share your face it could be cross referenced with other photos you appear in (like school photos, your family's social media, photos where you're in the background of) and use that to gather info about you.

Photos sometimes may also have location data attached to them, hidden in the [EXIF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exif) data depending on your camera app settings.

Here's what you should do:
- Never post photos of the outside world or photos that include a portion of it (like windows in a house)
- Never share your face
- Disable location tagging in your Camera app settings
- Carefully review your photos, videos and screenshots before sharing them

# Your IRL connections
Do not associate your accounts with the accounts of your family and real life friends (by following, friending or just mentioning them),
unless they also follow proper privacy practices.

If they follow you and this information is publicly visible, tell them to unfollow you.

Your IRL connections can easily lead to you.

# Place reviews
If you rated any place on Google Maps or a any other app or website, that's public information.
Each rating marks a place that you've been to and that makes a clue of where you might live.

Simply never put comments or ratings on physical locations and if you made any, delete them.

On Google Maps you can do it by going to **Your contributions** where you'll find your reviews, photos and other data.

# Facebook: The name stands for itself
Facebook is an absolute gold mine of private information.
I strongly advise against using it, but if you need to use it, then you must secure your account properly.

By default, all your information and activity on Facebook is public. That includes your friends, profiles you follow, posts you like and more.

Let's say that you liked a photo posted by your school. Well that very likely means you go to that school.

Even if your profile is fully locked down, your friends' profiles likely aren't.
If one of your friends also likes your school's post and you appear in their friend list that is also a clue.

Remember that even if you fully lock down your profile, that doesn't mean you will disappear from other people's lists.
You must disappear from them too.

Here's what you should do:
- Remove all your friends (you'll still be able to talk to them on Messenger and they likely won't even notice or care)
- Unfollow & unlike all profiles that are linked to you (ex. your school, workplace, local restaurant)
- Disable searching your profile by your phone number & email.
- Hide your profile from external search engines (like Google). Please note that it may take up to few months to disappear from search results.
- Make your profile private so it can't be publicly viewed (please note that you will still appear in friend lists unless you remove all friends)

All these things can be done within Facebook or its settings.

# Practice proper cybesecurity
Everyone says this, yet I still see people using passwords like *Cheese123456* and reusing them on every single website.

If you reuse a password, then a compromise of a single account automatically leads to other accounts. That's like a deadly master key to your entire online identity.

Remember that your accounts hold a lot of private information that isn't normally visible unless you are their owner. That's why you must secure your accounts properly.

Please, I beg you to:
- Use significantly different passwords. NEVER reuse them.
- Enable 2-factor verification, so even if someone has your password they still need your approval or a temporary code.
- Make your passwords complicated, like *75i_F@RteD+&it-sm3LLS_b@dPleASeHELP_952* instead of *Pancakes321*.
- Use a secure password manager so you don't have to remember all your passwords (best if it's an offline one that doesn't require an account).

# Everything leaks eventually
No matter how secure a company claims to be, they mess up eventually.
Google, Microsoft, Meta, Discord... they all were victims of hackers who extracted tons of personal user data and definitely will be again soon or later. 

You need to be careful with the non-public information you share with websites. Even if you can't see it publicly, it still sits in some database on some server out there.
Encryption? Forget encryption. Most of the time it's impossible or not cost effective for companies to encrypt user data.

You can check if you were a victim of a data leak on https://haveibeenpwned.com/ or a similar site. 
It scans the dark web and paste sites for your email.
You can also subscribe to recieve email alerts if you end up in a leak.

In summary, to protect yourself from data leaks:
- Use a leak detection service
- Assume everything will leak eventually. Limit the information you share.
- Use a temporary email if you need an account for some website, but don't necessarily care about it, like https://temp-mail.org 

# Stalk yourself
Since you probably learned a lot of scary skills from this guide, use them on yourself.
Imagine you're a bad actor trying to figure out who you are then use that to find your weak points and secure them.

- Google yourself
- Search your old messages and posts for private information, especially photos. Start with the oldest as you probably used to be dumber.
- Check your data in Google: **Manage your Google account** -> **Data and privacy**
- Analyze your social media profiles
- Find accounts you forgot about using tools like https://whatsmyname.me/

You can also use them on your friends and family to scare them into caring about privacy, but please do it wisely.
